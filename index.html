Meet Katy “the” Savage

My name is Katy Savage — but once I started competing, I became Katy “the” Savage. I picked up pickleball at the end of 2022, and at the time I was weak, overweight, and about as non-athletic as it gets. My love for the sport pushed me to train my way up to competing at the 4.5+ level against players half my age.

Along the way I lost 85 lbs and built real, visible strength — not just from playing, but from developing a strength-training approach from the ground up, built specifically for pickleball players. That approach became Pickle-Balance.

I got certified to coach in 2025, and since then I've helped over 200 players learn the game and move better, both on and off the court.

The Story Behind "Pace Yourself"

At 24 weeks pregnant, a COVID infection turned catastrophic. I was hospitalized, then given a stark choice: stay and face certain death for both me and my baby, or be intubated with only a 15% chance of survival. I chose to fight, made peace with the odds, and asked everyone I knew to pray.

I spent 9 days in a coma. By a miracle, I survived, but awoke to a body that had wasted away: unable to lift my head, my arms, or my legs.

Recovery was a battle every moment, but I was fighting for me and my daughter. In a moment of collapse and doubt, I prayed desperately and heard three words that reframed everything: “Pace yourself, girl.” That phrase carried me from lifting a heel half an inch off a hospital bed, to relearning to walk in physical therapy, to carrying my newborn daughter around the house doing modified leg lifts — to eventually discovering pickleball at Christmas 2022.

Pickleball wasn't the start of my recovery. It's where recovery became joy. And it's why Pickle-Balance exists: to help people move and get stronger at whatever pace is actually theirs.

“Some days all you can do is not give up, and that is enough. Even if you only make an inch of progress today, over time you will look back and see how far you've come.”

The Method: See, Repeat, Compete

Every weakness in a player's game traces back to one of three root causes — strength, technique, or mental/pressure. This is a repeatable process to diagnose and fix it.

1

See

Play live points and observe where exactly your game breaks down. We test hypotheses — knee bend, contact point, timing — to find whether it's strength, technique, or a mental block.

2

Repeat

Build corrective muscle memory through a drill progression: static reps first, then a moving ball, then live points — so the new movement survives contact with real play.

3

Compete

Put the corrected skill back into pressured, competitive play to confirm it holds under real conditions — not just in a quiet drill.

Who Pickle-Balance Is For

Most students are adults in their 50s and 60s, often discovering movement and competition for the first time as adults — rediscovering the fun of playing when you were a kid, but as an adult. The throughline isn't age, it's being new to athleticism.

Sarah

Loved the game but had no athletic background and didn't know where to start. Hip weakness was affecting her footwork — fixed with targeted hip strengthening. She calls it “life changing.”

Hahn

Fun-loving but stuck, dealing with knee and shoulder pain. The fix was positioning and mechanics, not more painkillers — his knee pain noticeably improved within five minutes of the right work.

Cat

Pickleball was her first sport, ever. Her timing was off — arriving at the ball too early or too late. Leg and hip strength work is closing the gap so she can cover the court with confidence.
